## Made for comprehensible input

The idea is simple: you learn a language by understanding a lot of it. Each video shows how many of its words you know, so you can pick material that is mostly familiar with a little new. One button starts learning the commonest words that would make a video comfortable to follow. The figure counts words you know. It is a guide, not a promise that you will understand every line.

## Private by design

- No accounts, no ads, no analytics. Your videos, transcripts and progress stay on your Mac.
- It goes online to download a speech model, and to check for a new version when you ask. That is all.
- The microphone is used only if you record yourself on a Shadow card, and the recording is deleted when the card ends.
- Your original files are only read, never changed or deleted.

## Finding videos to learn from

Kikimi never finds or downloads videos; it works on files you give it. If you have none, some places publish video you may download and study, each under its own licence: the [Internet Archive's Moving Image Archive](https://archive.org/details/movies) (some items come with subtitle files, which Kikimi can use instead of transcribing), [Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Videos), and [TED Talks](https://www.ted.com/about/our-organization/our-policies-terms/ted-talks-usage-policy) for personal use. "Creative Commons" is not one licence: check what each video and its subtitle file allow. This is not legal advice.
