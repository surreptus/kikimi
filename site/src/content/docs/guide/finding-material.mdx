title: Finding material
description: What makes a good video to learn from, and where to look for files you may keep.
---

Kikimi works on files you already have. It does not search for, stream or download anything from any website, so finding material is up to you. This page is about choosing well, and about the rules.

## What makes good material

- **You can follow most of it.** Slightly above your level works best: you understand the gist, and a few words in each sentence are new. If you understand almost nothing, pick something simpler. If you understand everything, pick something harder.
- **It has text.** A video that comes with subtitles in its own language (an `.srt` or `.vtt` file) imports in moments and needs no speech model. Without them Kikimi transcribes it, which takes longer and is less exact. See [importing videos](/kikimi/guide/importing-videos/).
- **It is clearly spoken.** Slow, clear speech with little music underneath gives the best transcript and the best sentences to hear again.
- **You would watch it anyway.** You will meet the same words many times, so a topic you like is worth more than the perfect level.

Your first video tells you the rest: its **words known** figure says how much of it you already understand, and **Next up** on Videos suggests which of your videos fits best.

## Where to look

Only use files you own or have the right to use. What you may do with a file depends on where it came from and on the law where you live, and the [terms](/kikimi/terms/) put that responsibility on you.

- **Your own recordings and files you bought**, such as a download you paid for with no copy protection.
- **Material shared for anyone to keep.** The [Internet Archive's video collection](https://archive.org/details/movies) and [Wikimedia Commons videos](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Videos) hold films, talks and clips whose pages state their licence. Check the licence on the item's page. On the Internet Archive, open an item's **Download options** to see its files, and look for subtitle files ending in `.srt` or `.vtt` beside the video.
- **A site's own terms.** Many sites allow watching but not saving. If a site doesn't offer a download, don't use a tool to take the file: that is usually against its terms, and Kikimi can't help with it.

## Finding age-appropriate material on the Internet Archive

The Internet Archive has **no age ratings and no family filter**, and nobody checks what is uploaded. A search for animation in Japanese brings up adult anime, and a search for German films brings up propaganda made for the Nazi government. So look at an item's page, and a few minutes of it, before you add it, and before a child watches it.

These searches narrow it to one language and leave out anything whose uploader tagged it as adult, erotic, horror, violent or silent. That only removes what is *tagged*, so it is a start, not a guarantee.

How to get more out of them:

- **Prefer institutions.** School districts, public television, libraries and community channels upload clearly spoken, family-suitable programmes. Look at who uploaded an item (the name under its title) and click through to see what else they have.
- **Films** are mostly older and in the public domain: mid-century classics, with some recent independent ones. They are not rated, so preview them. Many are wartime or violent even when untagged.
- **Subtitle files.** On an item's page, open **Download options** and press **Show all** to see its files, and look for `.srt` or `.vtt`. For a foreign-language film the subtitles are usually an English translation of the speech, which is no use here, and Kikimi refuses them. Check the subtitle language first, or add the video alone and let Kikimi transcribe it. Subtitles can also be stored *inside* a video file (an MP4 or MKV may hold several languages), which the Archive's file list does not show. Kikimi does not read those yet; see [importing videos](/kikimi/guide/importing-videos/). Spanish and French have a good number of items with subtitles in their own language; German and Japanese very few.
- **It only finds what is labelled.** The language and subjects are typed in by whoever uploaded the item, so an item labelled "spa" or left untagged will not appear. Try words in the language itself in the search box, such as *cuentos* or *dessin animé*.
- **Check the licence** on the item's page before you keep a copy.

## Collections made for learners

[COERLL](https://coerll.utexas.edu/coerll/materials/format/video-collection/), the Center for Open Educational Resources and Language Learning at the University of Texas at Austin, publishes video made for language learners under Creative Commons licences. The ones for languages Kikimi supports:

- **Spanish: [Spanish Proficiency Exercises](https://coerll.utexas.edu/coerll/oer/spanish-proficiency-exercises/)** (CC BY). Short clips of native speakers from Latin America and Spain doing everyday tasks, with a Spanish transcript you can show or hide.
- **Spanish: [SpinTX](https://spintx.org/)** (CC BY-NC-SA 3.0, as the site states). Hundreds of short interview clips with Spanish speakers in Texas, with captions and transcripts.
- **French: [Les Conversations Mises à Jour](https://coerll.utexas.edu/coerll/oer/les-conversations-mises-a-jour/)** (CC BY-SA, as COERLL lists it). Conversations between two French speakers, for intermediate and advanced learners.

These are built to be watched on their own sites. We could not find a link on them to save a clip as a file, and Kikimi needs a file, so check each site for a download option and for its terms. Where there isn't one, they are still good places to watch and listen. Credit and share-alike conditions are about passing material on, and each site's terms decide what you may do with a copy. German and Japanese have nothing comparable that we found.

## Courses and channels you can only watch

Courses and channels made for learners, such as Deutsche Welle's Nicos Weg for German, are very good for understanding. They are usually streamed, and Kikimi can't use a stream, so watch them where they are and use Kikimi for files you can keep.

## Audio

Kikimi imports video files today. Audio-only files, such as a podcast you have downloaded, are planned and not yet supported.
