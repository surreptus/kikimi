Open a video from **Videos**. The video is on the left, and its subtitles are on the right, following along as it plays. Kikimi remembers where you stopped and offers **Continue** or **Start over** next time.

## Words and their meanings

Point at a word in the subtitles, or move along a line with <kbd>←</kbd> <kbd>→</kbd>, and its meaning appears in the strip under the subtitles. Click it, or press <kbd>Return</kbd>, to keep its meaning open. <kbd>Esc</kbd> lets go.

Mark a word from the strip:

| Key | Does |
|---|---|
| <kbd>L</kbd> | **Learn**: add it to your reviews |
| <kbd>K</kbd> | **Known**: you already know it |
| <kbd>X</kbd> | **Ignore**: set aside a name or a mistake |

Words you are learning are underlined, and words you know have a dotted underline and a tint, so colour is not the only sign. The **Highlight** switch turns the marks off. Japanese lines show furigana when you point at them.

## Moving around

- Click a line to jump to it. <kbd>⌘←</kbd> and <kbd>⌘→</kbd> go to the previous and next line. <kbd>⌘↓</kbd> moves the keyboard into the subtitles.
- <kbd>Space</kbd> plays and pauses.
- <kbd>R</kbd> loops the current line until you press it again. While looping, a menu sets the speed (1×, 0.75× or 0.5×).
- <kbd>P</kbd> pauses after each line, to think or look a word up.

## Listening without reading

Press <kbd>H</kbd> to hide the subtitles. Each line becomes a plain bar, so you can't read the words, and a translation waits with its line. Press <kbd>S</kbd> on a line, or use its menu, to show just that line. Press <kbd>H</kbd> again to bring them all back.

## Translations

On macOS 26, a line can be translated on your Mac with <kbd>T</kbd>, or always. Translations stay hidden until you ask for one, so you listen first. On macOS 15 to 25 the translation controls are hidden.

## Fixing and saving lines

- <kbd>E</kbd> edits a line when the transcript is wrong. Kikimi finds its words again.
- A line's menu (the dots beside it) saves an audio or video clip of that line.
